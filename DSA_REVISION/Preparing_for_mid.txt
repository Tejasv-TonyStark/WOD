| Imaging Technique | Definition                                                                                                       | Workflow                                                                                                                    | Important Parts                                                        | Advantages                                                                      | Disadvantages                                              | Limitations                                                              | Applications                                                                                   |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Ultrasound**    | Uses **high-frequency sound waves** to create real-time images.                                                  | Electrical energy → Piezoelectric crystals → Sound waves → Tissue reflection → Echoes return → Processor → Image            | Probe, Piezoelectric crystals, Processor, Gel                          | Non-invasive, Real-time, Low cost, No ionizing radiation                        | Tissue heating, Cavitation                                 | Operator-dependent, Poor through air/bone, False +/− possible            | Pregnancy, Heart, Abdomen, DVT, Ligament/tendon injuries, Trauma                               |
| **X-ray**         | Uses **X-ray radiation** and differential absorption to form mainly 2D images.                                   | Cathode releases electrons → Electrons hit tungsten anode → X-rays produced → Pass through body → Detector → Image          | Cathode, Tungsten anode, X-ray tube, Detector                          | Fast, Cheap, Widely available, Excellent for bones                              | Ionizing radiation, Pregnancy concern                      | Poor soft-tissue contrast, 2D overlap                                    | Fractures, Chest, Dental, Mammography, DEXA, Fluoroscopy                                       |
| **CT Scan**       | Uses **multiple X-rays from different angles + computer processing** to create cross-sectional/3D images.        | Rotating X-ray tube → Multiple projections → Detectors → Computer reconstruction → Slices/3D image                          | X-ray tube, Detectors, Table, Computer, Contrast agent                 | Fast, High resolution, 3D imaging, Whole-body evaluation                        | Higher radiation, Contrast reactions, Expensive            | Limited soft-tissue contrast compared with MRI                           | Brain hemorrhage, Trauma, Lung disease, Complex fractures, Cancer, Abdomen                     |
| **MRI**           | Uses **strong magnetic field + RF waves** to image hydrogen protons and produce detailed soft-tissue images.     | Magnet aligns protons → RF pulse disturbs them → Protons relax → Energy signals released → RF coils detect → Computer image | Main magnet, Gradient coils, RF coils, Computer                        | No ionizing radiation, Excellent soft tissue, High resolution, Multiplanar      | Expensive, Long scan, Noise, Claustrophobia                | Some metal implants unsuitable, Motion artifacts                         | Brain, Spine, Ligaments, Muscles, Heart, Abdomen, Pelvis, Blood vessels                        |
| **PET**           | Nuclear medicine technique used to study **metabolic and physiological activity** using radioactive tracers.     | Radiotracer → Positron emission → Positron + electron → Annihilation → 2 gamma rays → Detector → Metabolic image            | Radiotracer/FDG, Detector ring, Scintillation crystals, PMTs, Computer | Functional imaging, Early disease detection, Whole-body imaging, Cancer staging | Radiation, High cost, Long procedure, Limited availability | Lower anatomical detail, False positives, Diabetes may affect FDG uptake | Cancer detection/staging, Alzheimer’s, Epilepsy, Parkinson’s, Cardiac viability, Infection     |
| **SPECT**         | Uses **gamma rays emitted by a radiotracer**, detected by rotating gamma cameras to create 3D functional images. | Radiotracer → Gamma emission → Rotating gamma camera → Multiple 2D views → Reconstruction → 3D image                        | Collimator, NaI scintillation crystal, PMTs, Gamma camera, Radiotracer | Functional imaging, 3D, Widely available, Cheaper than PET                      | Radiation, Long imaging time, Motion artifacts             | Lower resolution than PET, Limited anatomical detail                     | Cardiac perfusion, Epilepsy, Alzheimer’s, Stroke, Bone metastasis, Thyroid, Pulmonary embolism |
